## KEY INSIGHTS

### 1. The Retainer is a Fixed Cost Problem
- Retainer doesn't change with company size
- In-house costs scale with complexity
- Until in-house costs are high enough, retainer will always be more expensive

### 2. The Real Break-Even Point
- **~50 stakeholders**: CA and in-house are roughly equal cost
- **Below 50**: In-house is cheaper
- **Above 50**: CA is cheaper

### 3. Stage Makes It Worse
- Higher stages = higher retainers
- Series C retainer (₹407k) almost never justified except for 100+ companies
- Pre-seed retainer (₹60k) only justified for very high-volume companies

### 4. Geography Doesn't Matter Much
- All geographies show 64-68% scenarios where CA is MORE expensive
- The problem is the retainer model, not geography

## Conclusion

**CA Retainer is more expensive than in-house in 66% of realistic scenarios.**

The retainer model fundamentally doesn't work for small companies because:
1. Fixed cost (₹60k-₹407k) doesn't scale with company size
2. In-house hour costs scale with complexity
3. The break-even point is around 50+ stakeholders
4. Higher stages make the problem worse (bigger retainers)

**For 2 out of 3 companies, CA retainer is a bad deal.**

Only for very large companies (100+ stakeholders) or specific high-complexity scenarios does the CA retainer model make financial sense.
