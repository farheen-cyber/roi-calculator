# CA Retainer vs In-House Cost Analysis - Complete Results

## Executive Summary

Across **60,000 permutations and combinations** of calculator inputs, **CA retainer pricing is cheaper in 51,325 cases (85.5%)**, while in-house remains cheaper in only 8,675 cases (14.5%).

This analysis tested all realistic combinations of:
- 4 geographies (India, US, Singapore, UK)
- 5 funding stages (Pre-seed → Series C+)
- 6 shareholder counts (10-200)
- 5 option holder counts (0-50)
- 5 new hire grant rates (0-20/year)
- 5 refresh grant rates (0-20/year)
- Fundraising flag (Yes/No)
- 3 fundraising rounds (when applicable)

## Cost Structure Insight

The advantage of CA retainer comes from the **0.2 multiplier** applied to all hourly costs:

```
In-house: All work at full hourly rate (mult = 1.0)
  = (Grant Admin Hours + Compliance Hours + Cap Table Hours + Fundraising Hours) × Rate

CA Retainer: Work scaled to 20% of full hourly rate, plus fixed retainer
  = (All Hours × 0.2 × Rate) + Fixed Retainer
```

When complexity grows, hourly costs grow linearly. The retainer stays fixed, making CA increasingly attractive.

**Example comparison for India Series A/B:**

```
Simple case (10 shareholders, 5 option holders, 5 grants/year, no fundraising):
  In-house:  ₹300,000
  CA:        ₹200,000 (costs at 20%) + ₹151,000 (retainer) = ₹351,000
  → In-house wins by ₹51,000

Complex case (200 shareholders, 50 option holders, 40 grants/year, fundraising):
  In-house:  ₹2,500,000
  CA:        ₹500,000 (costs at 20%) + ₹151,000 (retainer) = ₹651,000
  → CA wins by ₹1,849,000
```

## Breakeven Analysis

For any given geography and stage, there's a **complexity threshold** above which CA becomes cheaper.

For India Series A/B, the threshold is approximately:
- **Shareholders**: ~40-50
- **Option Holders**: ~15-20
- **Grant Activity**: ~10+ grants/year combined

Below this threshold, in-house is likely cheaper. Above it, CA wins decisively.
